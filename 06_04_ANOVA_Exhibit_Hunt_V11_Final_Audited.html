ANOVA — Exhibit Hunt

Your mission: read each client brief as if it were the opening page of Exam 2. The question does not tell you which statistical table to trust. First reconstruct the business question, the unit of observation, the response, the grouping variable, and the decision at stake. Then consult the exhibits.
01 · Read and decodeIdentify who or what was measured, how many independent groups are compared, which outcome is numerical, and what the client really needs to decide.
02 · Hunt for relevant evidenceOpen the floating Statistical Appendix. Descriptives, ANOVA, Tukey comparisons, and deliberately unrelated tests sit together. An output can be statistically valid but irrelevant to this question.
03 · Interpret before decidingEvaluate assumptions, the F test and p-value, then post-hoc comparisons when appropriate. Separate evidence of some difference from evidence identifying which groups differ.
04 · Make the business callUse the stated practical threshold, cost, limitations, and uncertainty. Rejecting the null is not a substitute for a business recommendation.
Exam StyleRecognize the proper output, hypotheses, ANOVA conclusion, and basic interpretation in the same spirit as the exam.
ChallengeEvaluate tempting but flawed analyst claims, contrast exhibits, and explain the limitations of a reported result.
ConsultingAct as the advisor: synthesize imperfect evidence, challenge overconfident recommendations, and justify next actions.
Suggested workflow: Start with Exam Style without hints or answer checking. Review your feedback, then try Challenge and Consulting with the same client brief and appendix. Exam Style has 13–14 questions per case, including targeted Exam 2 transfer checkpoints; Challenge and Consulting each have 10 questions per case; switch cases from the tabs below.
