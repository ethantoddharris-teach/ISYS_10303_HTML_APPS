Neon Fork Collective — Business Case Overview

Neon Fork Collective (NFC) is a fast-growing network of chef-driven food trucks that operates near college campuses, music venues, festivals, and late-night entertainment districts across the central United States. NFC has built its reputation on creative menus, fast service, and a consistent guest experience, but rapid expansion has also created new questions about staffing, service reliability, customer satisfaction, and technology investments.

Before the next investor and operations review, COO Maya Chen has asked the analytics team to evaluate several recent initiatives and operating patterns. The results from those analyses have been mixed together in the Statistical Appendix. For each investigation, determine what information is relevant, select the appropriate output, and interpret the results in the context of the business decision being made. Focus on what the statistics mean for NFC rather than simply matching numbers to formulas.

Task 1 — Rush Bonus productivity review

As NFC expands into larger concert and festival events, leadership is increasingly concerned about whether crews can keep up with sudden surges in demand without sacrificing accuracy. To encourage stronger performance during peak periods, management introduced a Rush Bonus for selected kitchen crews. Worker productivity is measured by the number of completed customer orders per hour. Historically, participating trucks have averaged 72 completed orders per hour, which NFC uses as its established performance benchmark. Maya wants to determine whether the new incentive provides convincing evidence that average productivity has increased beyond that historical level. The result will influence whether NFC continues the bonus, changes staffing plans for major events, and considers extending the incentive to additional markets.
