Northstar Discovery Center · After Dark Study

Four experiences. One decision. ANOVA tells us where to look.

Northstar is deciding how to redesign its Saturday-night museum program. Four visitor experiences create very different staffing, reset, and equipment costs. Your job is to determine whether mean engagement differs anywhere across the four formats—and then use pairwise confidence intervals to decide which differences are both statistically convincing and operationally meaningful.

One-way ANOVA4 independent groupsα = .05Tukey pairwise intervalsPractical hurdle = 8 minutes
Study scenario. On five comparable Saturday evenings, arriving visitors were randomly assigned to one of four experiences: Standard Gallery, Meteorite Mission, Rover Challenge, or Cosmic Lab. Engagement time is the number of minutes a visitor remains actively involved before moving to the exit concourse. Each visitor experiences exactly one format. The operations team wants population-level evidence about which formats change mean engagement—not just a description of these particular visitors.
Director's hurdle: 8 minutes
Because the more elaborate formats cost more to staff and reset, leadership says a difference smaller than 8 minutes may be statistically real but too small to justify changing operations. That threshold is declared before looking at the results.
