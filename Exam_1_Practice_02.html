Autonomous delivery, AI operations, and decisions under uncertainty

NexDrop Campus Systems is a fast-growing technology company that operates autonomous delivery robots, smart pickup lockers, and AI-assisted support tools across university campuses and entertainment districts. COO Jordan Lee is preparing an operating review for investors after a semester of rapid expansion. Leadership wants to know whether a new route-optimization system is actually improving robot productivity, how confidently user-experience scores can be compared with corporate benchmarks, which delivery zones are the most predictable, and whether a new remote-recovery tool is reducing downtime. The analytics team has produced several statistical outputs, but they have been mixed together in one appendix. Your job is to connect each business question to the appropriate evidence, interpret the statistics in context, and make defensible operational decisions. Do not choose an output simply because a number looks familiar. First identify the variable, design, population question, and direction of the business claim; then use the statistical evidence that actually addresses that problem.

Task 1 — PathPilot productivity review

NexDrop recently activated PathPilot AI, a routing system that predicts pedestrian traffic, elevator congestion, weather interruptions, and battery demand in real time. Robot productivity is measured by the number of completed deliveries per active robot-hour. Before PathPilot, NexDrop used 28 deliveries per robot-hour as its established network benchmark. Jordan wants to know whether the new system provides statistical evidence that the true average productivity now exceeds that historical level. A positive result could support a wider software rollout and a new staffing model for remote fleet supervisors. A weak result could lead leadership to pause the expansion and investigate whether the observed gains are simply normal sample-to-sample variation.

Task 2 — App experience and expansion funding

After each completed delivery, customers may rate the app-and-robot experience on a 1-to-100 experience score, where higher values indicate a better overall experience. Leadership uses a score of 82.0 as the benchmark for approving additional lockers and robots in a deployment area. Because the collected surveys represent samples rather than every possible user, Jordan wants the recommendation to reflect both the observed average and the uncertainty surrounding that estimate. The board is especially interested in whether either area can demonstrate that its true average experience score is above the corporate benchmark rather than merely producing a sample average that happens to exceed 82.

Task 3 — Delivery-time consistency across operating zones

The goal is to distinguish normal operational variation from patterns that may require process changes.

Task 4 — VisionAssist remote-recovery pilot

When a robot becomes stuck or loses confidence in what its cameras are seeing, a remote operator may need to intervene. NexDrop piloted VisionAssist, an AI-assisted recovery interface that highlights obstacles and suggests a safe path for the operator.
