Northstar Museum · Visitor Experience Study

The Meteorite Mission

Use one museum decision to build a two-sample hypothesis test from the business question all the way to the final recommendation.

Northstar Museum has an underused gallery wing and is considering converting it into the Meteorite Mission, a staffed hands-on experience where visitors work through a simulated meteorite-recovery challenge. Unlike the existing Standard Gallery, the Mission requires facilitators, reset time between groups, specialized props, training, and ongoing maintenance. Leadership therefore does not want to expand it simply because a few visitors appear enthusiastic; they want evidence that the experience changes visitor behavior in a meaningful and repeatable way.

During four comparable evening events, eligible visitors were randomly assigned to either the Meteorite Mission or the existing Standard Gallery. Each visitor experienced only one format. The museum recorded the assigned Experience and the visitor's Engagement_Minutes from entering the experience until leaving it. The statistical question is whether population mean engagement is higher for the Mission. The operational question is stricter: even if the increase is real, is it large enough to justify the additional staffing and operating burden?
